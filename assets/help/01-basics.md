# Kakeibo basics

Kakeibo (kah-keh-boh) means "household account book". It was created in Japan in 1904 by Hani Motoko, the country's first female journalist. The idea is simple: when you write down what you spend and stop to think about it, you spend more carefully and save more.

## The four questions

1. **How much money do I have?** Your income for the month.
2. **How much do I want to save?** Decide this first, before you spend.
3. **How much am I spending?** Log each spend as it happens.
4. **How can I do better?** Reflect at the end of the month and choose one change.

## The four pillars

Every spend goes into one pillar. When you're not sure, ask: *do I need it, or do I want it?*

- **Needs**: things you can't do without. Groceries, bus fares, electricity, medicine.
- **Wants**: nice to have. Eating out, clothes you don't need, takeaway, gadgets.
- **Culture**: things that grow your mind or bring people together. Books, a course, a concert, a museum.
- **Unexpected**: surprises. A repair, a doctor's visit, a gift you didn't plan for.

### Ambiguous items

There's no single right answer. Choose once and stay consistent.

- **Phone bill**: a fixed cost if it's the same every month; Needs if it changes.
- **Lunch at work**: Needs if it's a simple meal you'd have anyway; Wants if it's a treat.
- **Birthday gift**: Culture if planned, Unexpected if not.
- **Netflix or Spotify**: a fixed cost (it's the same each month).
- **Tuk-tuk home in the rain**: Needs.

## Fixed costs and spending

**Fixed costs** are bills that are the same every month: rent, a loan payment, subscriptions. You enter them once in your plan and they're copied to the next month. You don't log them as spends, or they'd count twice.

Everything else you spend is **variable**, and that's what you log with the + button.

## The monthly cycle

**Plan → Log → Reflect.** At the start of the month you plan. During the month you log. At the end you reflect and choose one improvement for next month.
